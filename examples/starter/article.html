Where the price rise came from

Prices rose for twelve months in a row, but not evenly: two categories carried most of it.

The year in one line

Month to month the index moved by fractions of a percent, which is why the rise was easy to miss while it happened. Laid end to end, the year looks different.

Two things stand out. The rise accelerated after June, and it never paused: there is no month in which the index fell.

What drove it

A headline index is an average, and an average hides who pays. Split by category, the picture is lopsided:

  • Energy and housing account for more than half of the rise between them.
  • Food added a steady, smaller share every month.
  • Clothing pulled the index down slightly — the only category that did.

An average is a summary of people who are not average.

How we measured

Each category's contribution is its weight in the basket times its own price change. The contributions add up to the headline figure, which is what makes them comparable.

Limits

A basket is a model of spending, not anyone's actual receipt. A household that rents and drives felt the year differently from one that owns and walks — see the Consumer Price Index methodology of your statistics office for how its weights are set.
