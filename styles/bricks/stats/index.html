A stat is a KPI figure: a short label over a value, read at a glance and compared along a row. .stat sets the label small and muted, and gives the value tabular numerals: every digit takes the same width, so figures in a row line up digit by digit and a live value does not shift sideways as it updates.

Revenue, Q3
$2,840,112
<div class="stat">
    <div class="stat__label">Revenue, Q3</div>
    <div class="stat__value">$2,840,112</div>
</div>

The label and the value are direct children of the stat. A stat sets no surface, padding or alignment: it takes them from where it sits. In a panel it reads from the start; as the cells of a panel row it is centred and padded by the row.

In a panel row

Put .stat on the row's cells, or a stat inside a cell. On a phone a long row scrolls inside its own border, and each figure stays on one line. A value never wraps, not even at a space (95.4 M, +1.23 (0.76%)): in a box narrower than the figure, a row scrolls and a tile lets it overflow, rather than split it into what reads as two numbers.

Direction

.stat--delta-up and .stat--delta-down mark a change. An arrow before the value takes the role colour, --color-success up and --color-danger down; the figure keeps the text colour, because the semantic colours are signal colours, not text colours. Screen readers do not announce the arrow, so write the sign in the figure: the direction is in the words too.

Orders, week on week
+12.4%
Returns, week on week
−3.1%

The arrow is drawn in --stat-delta-color. Where a rise is bad news, as with costs or errors, set it on the stat:

<div class="stat stat--delta-up"
     style="--stat-delta-color: var(--color-danger)">
    <div class="stat__label">Support tickets</div>
    <div class="stat__value">+18%</div>
</div>

Emphasis

.stat--signal sets the value in --signal, bold: a figure that asks for attention, such as a threshold crossed. Let the label say why. Bold keeps the value large text, which the blue reads as on the light page and on a dark card.

Error rate, over the 1% limit
1.8%
