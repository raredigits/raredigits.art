The Value

Principles

Tips

Stop thinking about decorations, start thinking about the value.

The only thing that matters is the data your audience came for. Design, layouts, make-up and decorations are just your tools to highlight the value.

Imagine you have 30 secs to pitch your data. What would you say? What decision maker need to know? How would you present it? What would you highlight? What would you hide?

That's it. That's the only thing that matters. Delete the noise. Make your audience satisfied.

Bricks / Panels

css/modules/bricks/_panels.scss

The panel is the data surface: a KPI, a chart, a status, settings — content a system produced and a reader scans. A card is for narrative content; the rule for choosing between them is on the Building Blocks page.

A panel is a block with inner padding — 16px on every width. It paints no background and sets no text colour, so it works on any surface: the page, a card, a dark band. For the same reason it sets no --surface-current: the surface behind it passes through, and a chart in it (RareCharts 0.9.8_7 and later) paints its label plates with that surface (Surfaces). Blocks that hang into the page margin (pre, blockquote, .lead, the caption widths) stay inside it.

.panel.panel--bordered

Modifiers

.panel--bordered
The frame: a 1px border in --border-color with small rounded corners.
.panel--flush
No inner padding. For a panel docked inside another panel or a grid cell that already has its own.
Outer panel

A panel that hangs into the page margin — with .text-content-caption, .full-width-caption or .card-caption, as the outer panel above — steps its content back in by the same outdent: 32px on the side it hangs, so its text stays on the edge of the text column. No modifier is needed; the caption class is the cue. A panel grid or a panel row with a caption width steps in every cell the same way, on both sides: a panel, a card or a row cell takes 32px each side, so the first cell's text starts on the edge of the text column and the other cells match it. On a phone and on paper every such cell takes 16px each side, a card cell included; where the outdent is contained (in a card, a panel, a collapsible) a cell keeps its own padding; a .panel--flush panel stays flush.

Header, body, footer

Three optional parts give the panel a predictable frame. .panel__header lays out a title and its meta on one baseline, with a divider below; a heading inside it drops its own margins. A lone title spans the header, so .center on the panel centres it too. .panel__body takes the free height, so the footers of panels in one row line up. .panel__footer holds the source or a timestamp in small muted text, with a divider above. A panel with any of the three becomes a flex column; put loose text in the body, or it splits into one line per run.

Revenue
Q3 2026

$2.84M

Source: internal accounting
<div class="panel panel--bordered">
    <div class="panel__header"><h4>Revenue</h4><span class="note">Q3 2026</span></div>
    <div class="panel__body">…</div>
    <div class="panel__footer">Source: internal accounting</div>
</div>

Panel grid

.panel-grid arranges panels in as many columns as fit, each at least --panel-grid-min wide (14rem by default), and one column on a narrow screen. The panels of a row share its height. A cell may shrink below its content, so a chart in it redraws narrower instead of overflowing the grid.

To change how wide a column may get before the grid adds another, set --panel-grid-min on the grid: style="--panel-grid-min: 12rem" fits more, narrower panels in a row; a larger value fits fewer, wider ones.

Panel row

.panel__row is a bordered strip of cells side by side, divided by lines: KPI figures under a chart, a row of gauges. Each .panel__row-item takes an equal share and centres its text. It works inside a panel or on its own. On a phone a long row scrolls sideways inside its own border instead of widening the page. For KPI figures, make the cells stats.

For KPI figures, put .stat on the row's cells, or a stat inside a cell. On a phone a long row scrolls inside its own border, and each figure stays on one line. A value never wraps, not even at a space (95.4 M, +1.23 (0.76%)): in a box narrower than the figure, a row scrolls and a tile lets it overflow, rather than split it into what reads as two numbers.

Stacking mode

.panel__row--stack keeps the cells side by side from tablet width up and puts them one under another on a phone; the dividers turn with them. Use it for cells that must not shrink into a scrolling strip, such as charts. .panel__row--text-start aligns the cells' text to the start instead of the centre, so a chart's title in a cell reads like every other chart's.

Stats

css/modules/bricks/_stats.scss

A stat is a KPI figure: a short label over a value, read at a glance and compared along a row. .stat sets the label small and muted and the value bold, with tabular numerals: every digit takes the same width, so figures in a row line up digit by digit and a live value does not shift sideways as it updates. In a stat taller than its content, a tile in a panel grid or a cell of a panel row, the value sits at the bottom, so the figures of a row line up when one label wraps.

Revenue, Q3
$2,840,112
<div class="panel panel--bordered stat">
    <div class="stat__label">Revenue, Q3</div>
    <div class="stat__value">$2,840,112</div>
</div>

The label and the value are direct children of the stat. A stat sets no surface, padding or alignment: it takes them from where it sits. On a panel it is the panel itself (class="panel stat"), or the body of a panel with a header and a footer (class="panel__body stat", as in the panel grid), and reads from the start; as the cells of a panel row it is centred and padded by the row. It works on its own too, or on a card.

Direction

.stat--delta-up and .stat--delta-down mark a change. An arrow before the value takes the role colour, --positive up and --negative down; the figure keeps the text colour, because the semantic colours are signal colours, not text colours. Screen readers do not announce the arrow, so write the sign in the figure: the direction is in the words too.

Orders, week on week
+12.4%
Returns, week on week
−3.1%

The arrow is drawn in --stat-delta-color. Where a rise is bad news, as with costs or errors, set it on the stat:

<div class="stat stat--delta-up"
     style="--stat-delta-color: var(--negative)">
    <div class="stat__label">Support tickets</div>
    <div class="stat__value">+18%</div>
</div>

Emphasis

.stat--signal sets the value in --signal: a figure that asks for attention, such as a threshold crossed. Let the label say why. The value is bold, so it reads as large text, which the blue meets on the light page and on a dark card.

Error rate, over the 1% limit
1.8%

Chart frames

A chart's frame is a panel on the chart element itself — RareCharts draws into the element you give it and calls it the card. The chart keeps its own layout, clipping and spacing; the panel adds the padding and the frame, and the chart's height includes them. When a page composes more around a chart — a quote header, a row of figures — the panel wraps them all and the chart inside takes no frame of its own. Two places take no frame at all: a chart in a sidenote, which illustrates the text from the margin, and a chart in a panel row cell, which the row already frames.

Until RareCharts 0.9.8_7. A chart with no padding of its own — in a sidenote, a panel row cell or a panel body — can lose up to about 24px of its outermost axis and slice labels: the chart clips its drawing at the element’s edge. Give such a chart a little padding until then.

<div id="chart" class="panel panel--bordered text-content-caption"></div>

new RareCharts.Line('#chart', { title: 'Weekly active users' }).setData(data);

The panel's layout has zero specificity, so a layout utility on the same element (.grid) and the chart's own layout win whatever order the stylesheets load in. Its padding does not: like every component's, it wins over a spacing utility on the same element — use .panel--flush, or wrap the content, for other padding.

Renamed in v0.7.2

Before v0.7.2 the bordered panel and the panel row were misfiled under the card as .card-dashboard-bordered and .card-row-bordered / .card-row-bordered-item. Those names were removed with no alias: .card-dashboard-bordered is now .panel .panel--bordered (both classes: the padding is the panel's, the frame the modifier's), and the row is .panel__row / .panel__row-item. The migration table has the details.
