Tips

Stop thinking about decorations, start thinking about the value.

The only thing that matters is the data your audience came for. Design, layouts, make-up and decorations are just your tools to highlight the value.

Imagine you have 30 secs to pitch your data. What would you say? What decision maker need to know? How would you present it? What would you highlight? What would you hide?

That's it. That's the only thing that matters. Delete the noise. Make your audience satisfied.

Colors / Chart Tokens

css/modules/charts/_index.scss

The chart tokens name the colours and fonts a chart takes from the page: its series, its text, axes and gridlines, its range controls, its tooltip. Each has one --chart-* name. Most defaults point at a token the library already has, so a chart follows the page's palette, its surfaces and a project's overrides; three series, the tooltip's shadow and its type size keep RareCharts' own values.

RareCharts reads these names first, from its 0.9.8_7, and keeps its own values as fallbacks for a page without Rare Styles. A chart's own theme (an explicit theme, or darkTheme) still wins over the page.

The library's own pieces beside a chart do not read these names; they use the roles the chart tokens are made from. A legend swatch takes a series when you give it one, --swatch-color: var(--chart-series-1). A row of stats sets its labels in --text-color-light, which --chart-label-color points at by default. To change both, re-map the role; a re-mapped chart token changes only the chart.

Like every token they live on :root, outside the .rd gate: a chart in an island, or on a page without .rd, reads the same names. One of them names a colour the library cannot see there: see On a host's background.

Series

Six series colours, in the order a multi-series chart uses them: --signal, the main chart colour, then RareCharts' orange, green, yellow, violet and red. Where the library's palette has the hue, the token points at it. RareCharts 0.9.8_6 and earlier read no tokens and start with orange, then a light blue #0af, so their series 1 and 2 differ.

Re-map a series for every chart on :root, or for one chart on its container:

:root {
    --chart-series-1: var(--green);  /* every chart */
}

#revenue-chart {
    --chart-series-1: var(--purple); /* this one */
}

Surfaces

--surface-current is the background of the surface an element sits on. The page's is --bg-color (on paper it is white). A .card and a .card-inverted set their own and paint their background with it. A .panel paints no background and sets none, so the surface behind it passes through: a panel on a dark card sits on the dark card. --chart-bg follows the surface, so a chart's label plates hide the gridlines under them without showing a box (RareCharts 0.9.8_7 and later; earlier versions read none of the --chart-* tokens).

.card-inverted re-maps more than its text. Its border roles turn quiet on the dark surface, as they are on the light page: --border-color is --gray-mid-dark, --border-color-light a step closer to the surface, and --border-color-dark is light. So a frame, a panel's dividers and a chart's axes read without glaring. The chart tokens follow: light text and labels, and an active control in the darkest grey under a light label and border.

A .card inside a .card-inverted is a light surface again: it restores the text, link and border roles to their page values, and the chart tokens with them, so a chart in it reads as on the page. A project's own value on :root for one of the text, axis, grid or control tokens (the eight listed under A surface of your own) does not reach a .card-inverted or a .card in one: both resolve those tokens again from their roles. Restate the token on those surfaces. A role re-mapped on :root reaches the nested .card, but not the .card-inverted, which sets its own.

A surface of your own

Set --surface-current and paint with it. A custom property is resolved where it is declared, so a token made from another, such as --chart-bg from --surface-current, keeps its :root value inside your surface unless the surface restates it:

.report-band {
    --surface-current: var(--gray-mid-light);
    --chart-bg: var(--surface-current);

    background: var(--surface-current);
}

A dark surface also re-maps the text and border roles, and restates the chart tokens made from them, as .card-inverted does: --chart-text-color, --chart-label-color, --chart-axis-color, --chart-grid-color, --chart-control-text, --chart-control-border, --chart-control-hover-border and --chart-control-active-text, plus its own --chart-control-active-bg and --chart-control-fill.

On a host's background

In an island, or on a page without .rd, the library paints no page background: the block sits on the host's. --surface-current still says --bg-color, so a chart there would draw its label plates as light grey boxes on a background of another colour. Tell the island what it sits on:

<div class="rd" style="--surface-current: #faf0e6;
                       --chart-bg: var(--surface-current)">
    …a chart…
</div>
